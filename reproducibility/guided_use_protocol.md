# Guided Use Protocol

This protocol outlines the framework and fundamental rules applied specifically to the **Guided-LLM condition (Group 3)** to support effective, responsible, and learning-oriented interaction with Large Language Models (LLMs). The protocol frames the LLM as a fallible reasoning partner rather than an answer source and emphasizes preserving students' responsibility for reasoning and independent performance.

## 1. Orientation Session

Before beginning Topic 2, students in the Guided-LLM group completed a slide-based orientation to establish expectations for productive LLM use. The training framed the model as a fallible tutor rather than an answer source and provided examples of productive and unproductive prompts.

The orientation introduced the six fundamental rules described below and demonstrated how students could use the LLM to support explanation-seeking, stepwise help, active learning, verification, and ethical use during practice.

## 2. Weekly Reminders

To reinforce the initial orientation, students in the Guided-LLM group received weekly reminders throughout Topics 2–4. These reminders reinforced learning-oriented interaction patterns, including reasoning-focused help-seeking, concept tutoring, stepwise hints, active learning, verification and critical evaluation, and ethical use.

## 3. The Six Fundamental Rules (R1–R6)

### R1 — Process over the final answer

Use the LLM to understand strategies, assumptions, and intermediate steps rather than treating the final answer as the primary learning objective.

> **Student-facing instruction:** Ask how or why a method works, identify possible strategies, or show your own intermediate work. Preserve the reasoning work you will later need to perform without the model.

### R2 — Concept tutoring

Use the model to clarify and deepen conceptual understanding through definitions, intuition, analogies, alternative explanations, examples, applications, or connections to previously learned material.

> **Student-facing instruction:** If a textbook, lecture, or concept is unclear, ask the model to explain it in another way, give an intuitive explanation, provide an example, or connect it to something you already understand.

### R3 — Stepwise hints

When solving a problem, request incremental assistance rather than immediately asking for a complete solution. Appropriate help may include a first step, a hint, the next step, or feedback on a partial attempt.

> **Student-facing instruction:** Ask for the smallest amount of help needed to continue. When useful, ask the model to give one hint at a time and wait for your response before providing the next hint.

### R4 — Active learning

Use the LLM to generate additional practice, support self-quizzing, create summaries or study guides, or provide other activities that require the learner to actively retrieve or apply knowledge.

> **Student-facing instruction:** Ask the model to generate a new problem, quiz you on the topic, or help create a concise study guide. Attempt the activity yourself and use the model for feedback or further practice rather than simply requesting the answer.

### R5 — Verification and critical evaluation

Question, verify, or critically evaluate LLM outputs rather than accepting them uncritically. Treat model responses as potentially fallible and examine whether their reasoning, assumptions, calculations, and conclusions are consistent with course knowledge.

> **Student-facing instruction:** Check important claims, calculations, assumptions, and reasoning. If the model provides an explanation or solution, ask whether its logic is sound, identify possible errors, and compare the reasoning with course materials or other appropriate sources when needed.

### R6 — Ethical and no-help use

Avoid direct copying or answer-seeking misuse during practice and respect the no-help policy for quizzes and the final examination.

> **Student-facing instruction:** Use the LLM to support learning rather than to obtain answers for direct submission. During quizzes and the final examination, follow the study's no-help requirements and complete the assessment independently. Use the model only when its use is permitted during practice.
