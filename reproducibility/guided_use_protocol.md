# Guided Use Protocol

This protocol outlines the framework and fundamental rules specifically applied to the **Guided-LLM condition (Group 3)** to ensure students interact with Large Language Models (LLMs) effectively, responsibly, and as reasoning partners. 

## 1. Orientation Sessions
Before beginning Topic 2, students in the Guided-LLM group completed a slide-based orientation to establish baseline expectations for AI use. The training explicitly framed the model as a fallible tutor rather than an answer source and provided clear examples of productive and unproductive prompts. 

The orientation was conducted over two sessions:
* **First Orientation (August 6, 2025):** Focused on introducing the core concepts and responsible usage using the `student_guide.pdf` document.
* **Second Orientation (August 12, 2025):** Expanded on the practical application and prompting instructions using the `instructions.ppsx` presentation.

## 2. Weekly Reminders
To reinforce the initial orientation training, students in this group received weekly reminders throughout Topics 2–4. These reminders helped sustain a learning-oriented interaction pattern, consistently prioritizing explanation-seeking, stepwise help, verification, and ethical use during practice sessions.

## 3. The Six Fundamental Rules (R1–R6)

### R1 - Prioritize the reasoning process over the final answer
Use the LLM to understand strategies, assumptions, and intermediate steps. Do not treat the final answer as the main learning objective. This rule ensures students prioritize reasoning and intermediate steps over simply extracting final answers.

> **Student-facing instruction:** Ask how or why a method works, identify possible strategies, or show your own intermediate work. Preserve the reasoning work you will later need to perform without the model.

### R2 - Use the model as a concept tutor
Ask for definitions, intuition, analogies, alternative explanations, real-world applications, or links to prior knowledge.

> **Student-facing instruction:** If a textbook or lecture explanation is unclear, ask the model to explain the concept in another way and connect it to something you already understand.

### R3 - Request stepwise hints rather than a full solution
When stuck, ask for one hint, the first step, the next step, or feedback on a partial attempt. A complete worked solution, even when written step by step, does not preserve the student's responsibility for solving the problem.

> **Student-facing instruction:** Ask for the smallest help needed to continue. When useful, tell the model to wait for your response before giving the next hint.

### R4 - Check your reasoning
Present your logic, steps, or partial solutions to the model and ask it to verify your thought process and identify potential errors before moving forward.

> **Student-facing instruction:** Before concluding a problem, explain your logic to the AI and ask, "Is my reasoning correct?". Ensure you understand why a step works before proceeding.

### R5 - Active learning
Use the model for active practice after grasping a concept. Generate new practice problems, summarize key ideas, or conduct self-quizzing to build mastery.

> **Student-facing instruction:** Ask for a new problem or quiz, attempt it yourself, and then request feedback. Do not ask the model to solve the extra practice on your behalf.

### R6 - Validation and critical thinking
Understand that LLMs are not designed or optimized for precise mathematical calculations; their primary function is different, and they may easily make mistakes in calculations like combinations. 

> **Student-facing instruction:** You must explicitly check final calculations, such as C(12, 5), manually or with a calculator. Additionally, compare the logic behind the AI's guidance with textbook or classroom examples to ensure it is sound (e.g., verifying if a specific counting strategy suggested by the AI aligns with course principles). Comparing the AI's output with official sources is the best method of validation.
